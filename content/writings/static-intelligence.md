The model is an interface.

It lacks the dynamic intelligence living things have. An organism moves, wants things, corrects itself when it runs into the world. A language model does none of that on its own. It's a linguistic and semantic interface to a repository of human knowledge: what we've written, compressed and made queryable, built on the logic of language and on everything language presupposes.

On the other side there's an access layer and a very large archive behind it, with no autonomous being anywhere in the arrangement.

I have no contempt for what these models do. They are a very useful instrument. So I want to be accurate about what kind of thing they are.

---

## The Map That Doesn't Walk

A language model is a compressed map of everything that's been said. Every prompt is a coordinate. Every answer is whatever sits at that point.

The map is vast and detailed, but it doesn't move. Training ends and the map is printed. The weights stay as they were while you talk to it. What feels like the model getting to know you over a conversation is your earlier text being sent back in alongside your latest message, and the features sold as memory are stored notes pasted into the same place: a cold boot with a cheat sheet every single time.

Someone has to decide where to look. Between queries there's nothing: no thinking, no waiting, no wanting. A cold archive until the next request lands.

## Agency Is Borrowed

What the industry sells as "agents" confirms this. I can tell you what's inside one, because the parts are ordinary.

A person or a ticket supplies a goal. A harness, which is plain code, sends that goal to the model along with whatever context it has gathered. The model returns text, and some of that text is formatted as a request to use a tool: read this file, run this command, call this API. The harness runs the tool and appends the result to the transcript, stack trace included. Then it calls the model again with the longer transcript. This repeats until the task is marked done or a budget runs out.

Memory is a file or a database that the harness reads and pastes back in. A trigger is a cron job or a webhook. Error feedback is a failed test arriving as a string. The model never runs into the world. The harness carries the world to it, as text, one turn at a time.

Strip the loop and the interface sits still, waiting. Kill the process in the middle of a task and nothing misses it. Whatever dynamism you saw was lent by the system around it and, ultimately, by whoever designed that system.

## The Ceiling Is Grammatical

The repository is built from how we talk about the world, so it inherits form along with content: the presuppositions baked into language (subject and object, cause and effect, what counts as a thing) come preloaded.

What hasn't been said, or can't be said well, isn't on the map. Much of what a person knows never made it into a sentence, because the body held it and nobody needed to write it down. The model reproduces our blind spots along with our knowledge, frozen at the moment of training.

## Everything Is a Remix

Every output is a recombination of what already exists. No absolute generation. No pure creativity. The model interpolates between points it has seen.

But the space is large enough that interpolation lands in places nobody has visited: code that was never written, combinations nobody articulated, and at that scale the line between remix and novelty blurs.

And I don't see human creativity as anything else. There is nothing new under the sun, and much of what we call invention is recombination with judgment. A person who claims otherwise has mistaken the feeling of having an idea for the fact of having originated one. So I won't defend the human by pretending we pull things from nowhere.

Judgment is where the two come apart. The map pulls toward the center of what's been said, because that's where the weight of the archive sits. Taste requires someone willing to be wrong in a specific direction, and being wrong has to cost that someone something.

## Novelty Without Origin

The line that does hold runs between producing something new and having a reason to look for it.

The machine can generate the unprecedented. What it can't do is want to. No hunger, no curiosity, no question that starts from within. The question always comes from outside. So does the direction. The stakes always sit with someone else.

## Who Wrote the Hunger

The strongest objection comes from my own side.

I don't believe anyone chooses what they want. The will wills itself. Disposition was never yours to choose, and I've said so at length. Your hunger was written by natural selection, your curiosity by a brain you didn't design, your ambition by a childhood you didn't pick. So the organism's drives came from outside too. Evolution set its goal the way an engineer sets an agent's. Why should one count as origin and the other as borrowed?

I grant all of it. Nobody authored their own want. If origin means uncaused cause, then nothing has it, and I'm not smuggling a soul in through the back door to rescue the distinction. There is no metaphysical solution here either.

What I mean by origin is the place where the want is held and paid for. Evolution wrote the hunger and left. Nobody stands outside the organism holding its goal, ready to restate it on the next turn. The animal has to keep itself going: it survives by turning over its own matter, and the day the turnover stops, it is dead. Hans Jonas called this needful freedom: the living thing's freedom from any particular stretch of matter comes bundled with its need for matter, always, on pain of ending. Remove everything around an organism and it goes looking for food. Remove everything around a model and nothing happens, and it costs the model nothing.

The lamp cannot light itself, but it is a lamp. It moves. It burns. A determined organism is still burning, and it burns on its own account, whoever lit it. Its want is unchosen and also inescapable, and owning that want is the only sovereignty available to anyone. A vessel ends, and the ending closes it into a shape nothing else will have. The want runs out against that ending, which is what gives it weight.

My one commitment is to not lie about what can be known, so here is the limit of all this. No one has ever verified another interior, and I can't verify the absence of one in a model. My claim rests on what can be observed: where the goal is kept, what happens when the loop is removed, who loses something when the task fails. It also tells you what would change my mind. Build a thing that maintains itself, that can truly end, whose goals no one outside it holds or can restate, and the word static no longer applies to it. A budget limit written into a harness doesn't qualify, because the loss lands on whoever is paying for the compute. That thing would also no longer be the model I'm describing.

## The Tell

Some people already know how this ends.

The tell is always the same: certainty about destination. In January 2025 Sam Altman wrote, "We are now confident we know how to build AGI as we have traditionally understood it." Ray Kurzweil has held to 2029 for human-level AI and 2045 for the Singularity, when we merge with the intelligence we've made. The doomers run the same calendar and swap the ending. The masses were offered heaven, the intellectuals were offered History, and engineers are now being offered the Singularity. Each comes with a date, a chosen few who can read the signs, and a reason why present costs are justified by what's coming.

Models will improve, and the map will get larger and sharper. What I reject is the slide from a better map to a walker, as if enough detail would eventually make the paper stand up. Nobody has shown that. People assert it with the confidence of those who've taken the substance, and it absorbs every counterexample: each failure is a matter of scale, and the next model settles it.

Follow the leverage and the certainty makes more sense. If the machine is on its way to becoming a being with its own ends, then the people steering it are midwives to something larger than themselves. If it's an instrument, then they are people holding an instrument, and everyone gets to ask what they're doing with it and on whose behalf. An interface to the whole human archive is an enormous concentration of power, and it answers to whoever holds the loop. Talking about the model's future agency keeps attention away from the agency that already exists on the other side of it.

## What It Leaves Us

That's static intelligence: an instrument for querying and recombining knowledge, capable of generation but never of origin.

The question it can't answer for me is the one I was already stuck with: _how do I live?_ Meaning was never stored in the archive, because it's an exploratory process, something done by a creature on its way to ending. The map can tell me everything that's been said about that. Where to walk is still mine to decide, on a want I didn't choose, with my own body as the stake.
