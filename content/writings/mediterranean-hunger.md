> _Yo soy mis circunstancias y el pasado interminable que concedió mi existencia, y hasta que no los redima, viviré una vida que nunca fue mía, dormido en el sueño eterno que precede, desconocido, a la muerte._

> Yo soy mis circunstancias, mi pasado y mi historia, y hasta que no sean salvadas, jamás redimiré mi vida y el legado heredado.

_Raw notes, draft. Written late, one of those nights._

## The two spirits

- Spain has always been understood as passionate, romantic, Dionysian. The pleasures of life over the idea of freedom, which comes close enough.
- Germany's spirit is to keep redefining what Germany is. Feudal lands, then 19th-century unification, then Prussia with its war, order, discipline and appetite for the authoritarian.
- Nietzsche: the question "what is German?" never dies out among Germans. The identity is the search itself.

## Longing in two directions

- Spain carries a melancholy for lost greatness and the desire to reclaim it. The country is always on the brink of getting some great lost thing back.
- Quixote is the founding figure, a man reclaiming a golden age that never existed. The German Romantics (Tieck, the Schlegels, Schelling) read him as a tragic hero.
- The German poets knew the limits of their own culture. They had to draw it themselves, leave, and extract it from elsewhere. In that sadness they found Italy, southern France, Spain.
- _Sehnsucht_ points forward and elsewhere. _Añoranza_ points backward. I carry both.
- Much of the romantic image of Spain was painted by northerners who needed a warm country to dream about.

## Language and upbringing

- Blood is one inheritance: the collective dreams, anxieties, delusions and ambitions of both cultures. Circumstance is another: a Spanish family at home, a German school that felt stiff, disciplined, almost military.
- German holds the verb until the end of the clause, so you have to hold the whole thought before it lands.
- Spanish splits _ser_ and _estar_, what you are and how you are right now. A grammar for passing states takes moods and moments seriously.
- _Se me cayó_: things happen to you in Spanish.

## Quiet intensity

- I've always had a quiet intensity. In Spain the intensity is loud. Italians share it: _passione_.
- Call it Mediterranean hunger, the love of life.
- Mine may be the same hunger held in by the German side.

## A map of temperaments

- Draw the spirits of peoples across west and east, north and south, and the same pattern repeats, because that's how people are. It's in all of us and shows up differently in each place.
- Earlier versions (Montesquieu's climate theory, Kant on national character, Hippocratic temperaments) ranked peoples. This one maps them.

## Why write it

- Having traveled and lived where I have, I've learned to see beauty everywhere: in human work and in these traditions.
- Spain tends to be down on its luck. It always has been. Its beauty is still worth honoring.
- I'm not a patriot and don't talk in patriotic terms. I happen to be born here. Even if I hadn't been, I would have said my spirit is a Spanish spirit.

## La tercera España

- Machado, _Campos de Castilla_: "Españolito que vienes al mundo te guarde Dios. Una de las dos Españas ha de helarte el corazón."
- My mother: "con saber con quiénes no estás, ya sabes con quién estás." Identity by negation, the same move Germany makes asking what German is.
- I don't wish to be ruled. Not left, not socialist, not fascist, not conservative. This country deserves better than its fabricated duality.
- During the Civil War a handful refused both sides: Ortega, Marañón, Pérez de Ayala, Madariaga, Chaves Nogales. Most ended in exile.

### What to take from them

- **Ortega**: the mass-man is an attitude, the self-satisfied spoiled child who demands rights and owes nothing. It cuts across class; a banker or a minister can be one. The masses are ruthless, victim and perpetrator on many layers, and they need to know better or the elite keeps playing them. Discard his hope that they'd follow select minorities.
- **Marañón**: liberalism as conduct, more than politics. Be willing to come to terms with those who think otherwise, and never accept that the end justifies the means: "son los medios los que justifican el fin."
- **Chaves Nogales**: "pequeño burgués liberal, ciudadano de una república democrática y parlamentaria." Free development of the personality, sovereign independent judgment. Said he'd done enough to be shot by either side, and left Spain in 1936.
- **Madariaga**: _Englishmen, Frenchmen, Spaniards_ (1928) compared national temperaments, the same map as above. Discard his faith in international institutions taming nations.

### Where I stand

- The word liberal has lost its meaning; a lot of rain has fallen since them. Their liberalism was a temperament: the individual before the party, the many educated over the many flattered.
- Humanist first, individualist. I want the government to leave me alone and the masses to know better.
- The epistemic side (why people stop thinking, beliefs as rafts) went into [The Perpetual Illusion](https://app.notion.com/p/314143f2826081c8b6aeed8e91342a09).

## Saving the circumstance

- Ortega, _Meditaciones del Quijote_ (1914): "Yo soy yo y mi circunstancia, y si no la salvo a ella no me salvo yo." Saving something meant understanding it until it reached the fullness of its meaning.
- My version: "Yo soy mis circunstancias, mi pasado y la historia que precede, y hasta que éstas no sean redimidas, mi vida no se salvará del sueño eterno." It adds time: Benjamin's past waiting for redemption, Calderón's dream.
- Leaving settles nothing. The circumstance travels with whoever leaves.
- Spain is an unfinished country. It deserves to be saved by people with more judgment than the psychopaths and sophists who march under false banners and false idols.
- Saving the circumstance runs from near to far, from the people closest to you out to the country. The work is the same at every scale: resisting a world that makes everyone smaller, isolates people and cuts the roots that tie a person to a place.
- Simone Weil, _L'enracinement_ (1943): rootedness is perhaps the most important and least recognized need of the human soul. Whoever is uprooted uproots others.
- The place, culture, era, language and temperament you are born into are arbitrary. Seeing that clearly doesn't cancel the debt to them.
- Movement can be part of the shape as long as it has a root and somewhere to return to.
