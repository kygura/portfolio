Corposlop: the output of any product, mostly games but not only, that has passed through an executive board whose job is to eliminate risk. No one on that board has taste. Taste implies a point of view, and a point of view is a liability: it can be wrong, it can alienate a segment, it can fail to test well. So every decision gets pulled toward the one thing that can't be blamed: the median.

The median is safe because it's already been validated by someone else's success. It's Fortnite's monetization plus Marvel's IP plus whatever engagement loop performed best in the last quarterly report. Corposlop is often extremely competent. Frame rate's fine. Onboarding is frictionless. It's bad in the sense of absent, the work of a committee optimizing against downside with no author behind it.

The people on that board are playing their own game correctly. In March 2024 JB Perrette, who ran streaming and games at Warner Bros. Discovery, [told a Morgan Stanley conference](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/warner-bros-reiterates-plans-to-increase-focus-on-free-to-play-and-live-service-games) that relying on triple-A console releases "just makes it very volatile." From his chair that is the honest diagnosis. A hit is a variance problem, and an executive gets paid to remove variance. I have no quarrel with the man. What interests me is what his incentive does to a game on its way through the building.

## Greenlight

Sony planned 12 live-service games by 2025. [PC Gamer counts eight of them cancelled](https://pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/sony-games-boss-says-company-will-bravely-keep-sending-live-services-over-the-top-despite-cancelling-8-of-the-12-ones-planned-for-2025-and-wiping-out-most-of-destiny-2s-developers/), Concord among them. The number came first, and the games were recruited to fill it.

[Concord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concord_(video_game)) launched on August 23, 2024, at $40, in a genre dominated by free-to-play rivals. One of its designers said it had been in development for about eight years. It peaked at around 700 simultaneous players on Steam and sold an estimated 25,000 copies in its first week. The servers went dark on September 6 and Sony refunded everyone. In October Sony closed the studio, and Hermen Hulst [explained](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/playstation-has-closed-concord-developer-firewalk) that "certain aspects of Concord were exceptional, but others did not land with enough players." Read that sentence for a person. It has a target and a shortfall, and I can't find anyone in it who wanted anything.

The failure exposes the trick in the median. A validated idea is a crowded trade. By the time the success is visible enough to convince a committee, everyone else reading the same quarterly reports has seen it too, and the money has already been made by whoever took the risk when it looked stupid. The board believes it is buying safety, and it pays full size for the worst entry in the market.

Warner's version began the same way. Rocksteady came off Arkham Knight in 2015 and its founders, Jamie Walker and Sefton Hill, were prototyping a multiplayer puzzle game codenamed Stones. Around the end of 2016 [they told staff](https://gameinformer.com/news/2024/06/06/a-new-report-details-how-a-troubled-development-led-to-suicide-squad-kill-the) they were switching to Suicide Squad, a film property Warner wanted to build on, as a live-service game. The studio had no multiplayer experience. It grew from about 160 people to more than 250 to learn on the job.

## Playtest

The second checkpoint is where a game gets to hear no. Rocksteady employees questioned the design, and Bloomberg's reporting describes a culture of toxic positivity, with [criticism shut down](https://www.gfinityesports.com/news/suicide-squad-devs-knew-the-game-would-fail) and staff told the game would pull through at the last minute, as the Arkham games had. Whoever is paid to believe in the slate cannot afford to hear the people building it.

Sony's stated remedy runs into the same wall from the other side. Hulst [told the Financial Times](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/107298/playstation-boss-on-concord-live-games-we-want-to-fail-early-and-fail-cheaply/index.html): "I don't want teams to always play it safe. But when we do fail, I would like for us to fail early and cheaply." He added: "We have since put in place much more rigorous and more frequent testing." I take him at his word about the first part. The second undoes it. A test tells you how a stranger responds to the thing in the first hour, measured against what that stranger already knows he likes. Run it more often and earlier, and you hand the median a veto at every stage of a game's life. You will fail more cheaply. You will also kill the strange thing while it still looks like a mistake, which is what every strange thing looks like early on.

## Monetization review

Then the bill arrives, and the last of the game's reasons get replaced. Suicide Squad slipped from 2022 to 2024. As costs climbed, the priority moved toward recovering them. Two former developers, Axel Rydby and Johnny Armstrong, [talked to Bloomberg about it in 2026](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/suicide-squad-kill-the-justice-leagues-development-got-so-bad-that-it-almost-turned-its-devs-away-from-the-industry-for-good-i-could-feel-myself-coming-apart-at-the-seams/). The questions in the room had become "How many players can we reach with the feature?" and "How can we twist this design into something that can be more replayable?"

Rydby, one of the game's directors, [gave the best description of corposlop I have read](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/suicide-squad-development-felt-like-i-wasnt-making-games-anymore-says-director/). "I was following a spreadsheet," he said, "some elusive marketing-analysis spreadsheet that no one could present clearly." And: "That's when I started feeling like I wasn't making games anymore." Nobody could present it clearly because nobody had to stand behind it. The authority belonged to a document, and a document can't be argued with or fired. The game [reportedly lost Warner $200 million](https://gameinformer.com/news/2024/06/06/a-new-report-details-how-a-troubled-development-led-to-suicide-squad-kill-the).

## A mind you can locate

Take those two questions to [Outer Wilds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Wilds). It began in late 2012 as Alex Beachum's master's thesis at USC, built with about 30 other developers, students among them. Beachum wanted "the primary goal being the exploration itself." The sun goes supernova every 22 minutes and the loop resets. Knowledge is the main form of progression.

How many players can we reach with this feature? Fewer, obviously. How can we make it more replayable? You can't. What you learned can't be unlearned, so the game is spent once you've understood it. Every question in that meeting argues against this design, and on its own terms every question is correct. Someone was willing to be wrong in that specific direction for years. It raised $125,000 on Fig. Annapurna bought out the investors and pushed for a more polished, commercial game, and the release slipped to 2019. Then it took Best Game at the BAFTAs.

What makes something corposlop, whatever its business model, is the absence of a mind you can locate. A game made by one obsessive designer with bad taste is more alive than a game made by fifty good designers whose judgment was overridden at every checkpoint by a board that has none.

## The hard case

Large companies do ship authored work, and the exception tells you the mechanism. Elden Ring has sold more than 25 million copies, and its difficulty is exactly what that meeting would have sanded off. Hidetaka Miyazaki [told the Guardian](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/lowering-elden-rings-difficulty-would-break-the-game-itself-says-miyazaki): "If we really wanted the whole world to play the game, we could just crank the difficulty down more and more. But that wasn't the right approach." Turning it down, he said, "would break the game itself." That is a man declining the first question from Rydby's meeting, in public, with the answer already priced in.

He can do that because he has been [president of FromSoftware since 2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidetaka_Miyazaki). As of August 2022, Kadokawa owned [69.66% of the company, with Tencent and Sony holding the rest as minorities](https://gematsu.com/2022/08/tencent-and-sony-interactive-entertainment-collectively-acquire-30-34-percent-of-fromsoftware). The person with the taste runs the checkpoints. Good taste has no rights in any boardroom, and no one is coming to protect the author on principle. Taste survives where it holds leverage: a track record, a title, control of the review, or an audience so devoted that overruling it costs more than obeying it.

Having a mind at the top guarantees nothing by itself. Rocksteady had one. Hill scrapped large parts of the script, staff waited weeks or months for him to review their work, and he and Walker left to found Hundred Star Games before the game shipped. A locatable mind can be wrong and can be a bottleneck. It can also lose the argument with its own balance sheet long before it notices. Authorship earns nothing except the possibility of being answerable, and that is the whole of what I'm asking for. When Outer Wilds fails you, you know where to send the complaint.

## The corp

This is why corposlop reliably has the texture of a cyberpunk megacorp's output: that's what it is. A cyberpunk corp produces product that mimics culture closely enough to extract value from people who wanted the real thing, and that flattening was always the dystopia. The fiction got the mood right and overestimated the malice. Everyone in that building is reducing variance at his own checkpoint, and the absent mind is what adds up.

I don't expect the board to change, and I wouldn't bother asking it to. It answers to quarterly numbers and it will keep choosing an average of all directions, because that's the game it was handed. The rules are visible and so are the exits. Rydby and Armstrong left Rocksteady, and PC Gamer reports they've made an RPG deckbuilder called Secret of Circadia with a Kickstarter asking for $11,404. Set that beside $200 million. I have no idea whether it's good, and it doesn't matter for this argument: at that price there is no spreadsheet between them and the work, and whatever is wrong with it will be theirs. Taste is expensive inside the building because it has to beat everyone's fear there. Outside, it costs what you can afford to lose, and most people who say they want to make something have never checked that number.
